Course positioning

Taught by Ron Dror, CS 279 covers the three themes of structure, dynamics, and design in computational biology. It goes from protein structure prediction to protein design, drug screening, molecular dynamics, cell simulation, and microscopy image analysis — a core course for understanding AI4Bio in the AlphaFold era.

What it covers

  1. Protein structure prediction: AlphaFold, Rosetta, homology modeling
  2. Protein design: directed evolution, generative design
  3. Drug screening: docking, virtual screening, ML scoring
  4. Molecular simulation: MD, free energy, enhanced sampling
  5. Cell simulation: networks, spatial modeling
  6. Microscopy image analysis: segmentation, tracking, super-resolution
  7. Structure determination: crystallography, cryo-EM
  8. Project

Prerequisites

CS 106A; introductory biology/biochemistry.

Assessment & workload

Assignments plus a project, possibly with paper discussion. Check the current syllabus for details.

Who it's for

Computational biology, drug discovery, and AI4Science; biology/chemistry backgrounds moving into computing.

Study advice

Brush up on molecular biology first. Pair with CS 273B for machine learning.

Official course description

Computational techniques for investigating and designing the three-dimensional structure and dynamics of biomolecules and cells. These computational methods play an increasingly important role in drug discovery, medicine, bioengineering, and molecular biology. Course topics include protein structure prediction, protein design, drug screening, molecular simulation, cellular-level simulation, image analysis for microscopy, and methods for solving structures from crystallography and electron microscopy data. Prerequisites: elementary programming background (CS 106A or equivalent) and an introductory course in biology or biochemistry.
