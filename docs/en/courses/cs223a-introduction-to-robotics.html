Course positioning

CS 223A (ME 320) is taught by Oussama Khatib and is the classic introduction to robotics. It covers spatial transformations, forward/inverse kinematics, Jacobians and statics, dynamics, trajectory planning, and classical control, with demos on physical robot platforms/simulation.

What it covers

  1. Spatial transformations: rotation matrices, homogeneous transforms, quaternions
  2. Forward/inverse kinematics: DH parameters, analytical/numerical IK
  3. Jacobians and statics: velocity mapping, force transmission
  4. Dynamics: Lagrangian, Newton-Euler
  5. Trajectories and planning: interpolation, obstacle avoidance, RRT
  6. Control: PID, computed torque, impedance control
  7. Project/lab

Prerequisites

Matrix algebra; programming basics.

Assessment & workload

Assignments plus a project/exam. Check the current syllabus for details.

Who it's for

Robotics beginners; people interested in manipulation, control, or embodied AI; ME/EE/CS backgrounds all fit.

Study advice

Brush up on linear algebra and rigid-body transforms first. Pair with CS 225A for hands-on work.

Official course description

Robotics foundations in modeling, design, planning, and control. Class covers relevant results from geometry, kinematics, statics, dynamics, motion planning, and control, providing the basic methodologies and tools in robotics research and applications. Concepts and models are illustrated through physical robot platforms, interactive robot simulations, and video segments relevant to historical research developments or to emerging application areas in the field. Recommended: matrix algebra.
