## Course positioning

CS 229M (STATS 214) is the most mathematically demanding ML course, aimed at graduate students who want to do theory. It does not teach model tuning; it trains you to analyze modern large-scale nonlinear models with concentration inequalities, uniform convergence, and optimization theory.

## What it covers

- **Concentration inequalities**: Hoeffding, Chernoff, McDiarmid
- **Generalization theory**: uniform convergence, VC dimension, Rademacher complexity
- **Non-convex optimization**: gradient descent, saddle points, landscape
- **Implicit regularization**: overparameterization, double descent, margin stability
- **Unsupervised learning and domain adaptation**: representation learning theory
- **Modern model analysis**: approximation and generalization of Transformers/deep networks

## Assessment & workload

Assignments plus a final/project, mostly proofs and paper reading. Check the current syllabus for details.

## Who it's for

People who want to do ML theory, a PhD, or rigorous analysis; also suitable for math/statistics backgrounds moving into ML.

## Study advice

Brush up on real analysis and probability first. Don't expect to understand it in one pass — work through the notes and papers repeatedly.

> How do we use mathematical thinking to design better machine learning methods? This course focuses on developing mathematical tools for answering this question.
