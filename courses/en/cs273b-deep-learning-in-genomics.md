## Course positioning

Taught by James Zou, CS 273B is one of the most popular AI4Bio courses. It first covers deep learning and genomics background, then applies supervised, unsupervised, and generative models to biomedical data, emphasizing biological insights extracted from models, and ends with a team project on real data.

## What it covers

- **DL basics**: CNNs, RNNs, Transformers, self-supervision
- **Genomic data**: sequencing, expression, variants, single-cell
- **Supervised prediction**: disease risk, drug response, regulation
- **Unsupervised/generative**: clustering, VAEs, diffusion, protein design
- **Interpretability**: attribution, motifs, attention
- **Paper discussion and guest lectures**
- **Real-data project**

## Assessment & workload

Paper discussion plus a team project and assignments. Check the current syllabus for details.

## Who it's for

Bioinformatics, computational biology, medical AI, and AI4Science; biology backgrounds moving into ML.

## Study advice

Take CS 229 first. Pick a dataset with a clear biological question; don't just chase accuracy.

<summary>Official course description</summary>

> Recent breakthroughs in high-throughput genomic and biomedical data are transforming biological sciences into &quot;big data&quot; disciplines. In parallel, progress in deep neural networks are revolutionizing fields such as image recognition, natural language processing and, more broadly, AI. This course explores the exciting intersection between these two advances. The course will start with an introduction to deep learning and overview the relevant background in genomics and high-throughput biotechnology, focusing on the available data and their relevance. It will then cover the ongoing developments in deep learning (supervised, unsupervised and generative models) with the focus on the applications of these methods to biomedical data, which are beginning to produced dramatic results. In addition to predictive modeling, the course emphasizes how to visualize and extract interpretable, biological insights from such models. Recent papers from the literature will be presented and discussed. Experts in the field will present guest lectures. Students will be introduced to and work with popular deep learning software frameworks. Students will work in groups on a final class project using real world datasets. Prerequisites: College calculus, linear algebra, basic probability and statistics such as CS 109, and basic machine learning such as CS 229. No prior knowledge of genomics is necessary.
