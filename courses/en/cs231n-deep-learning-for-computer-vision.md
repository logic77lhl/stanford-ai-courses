## Course positioning

CS 231N is the standard entry point to visual recognition and emphasizes the full implement–train–debug loop. Its assignment design (A1–A3 plus a final project) has been borrowed by many courses, and for many people it is the first place a neural network actually runs.

## What it covers

- **Image classification**: kNN, linear classifiers, losses and optimization
- **Neural networks**: backpropagation, multilayer networks, activations
- **CNN architectures**: AlexNet, VGG, ResNet, efficient networks
- **Training engineering**: initialization, normalization, regularization, learning rates, data augmentation
- **Visualization and interpretability**: saliency maps, feature visualization, adversarial examples
- **Detection and segmentation**: R-CNN family, YOLO, semantic/instance segmentation
- **Frontier topics**: video, generative models, multimodality, Transformers
- **Final project**: usually a real vision task

## Assessment & workload

Three programming assignments plus a final project; the workload is heavy but worth it. Check the current syllabus for details.

## Who it's for

People aiming at CV, multimodality, robot perception, or autonomous driving; engineers who want the low-level implementation skills behind deep learning; prospective vision PhD students.

## Study advice

Hand-write BatchNorm in A2 and the Transformer in A3 instead of copying. For the final project, don't just chase SOTA — a small, well-explained error analysis is more convincing.
