## Course positioning

CS 25 is one of Stanford's most popular 1-unit seminars, with speakers from OpenAI, Anthropic, Google DeepMind, NVIDIA, and elsewhere. There are no assignments — attendance is enough — and lectures are streamed and recorded, making it a fast way to scan the frontier.

## What it covers

- **Transformer architecture**: attention, positional encoding, variants
- **LLMs**: pre-training, alignment, reasoning, agents
- **Generative models**: images, video, audio, 3D
- **Robotics and embodiment**: VLAs, world models
- **Scientific applications**: medicine, neuroscience, materials
- **Industry perspective**: models, compute, products

## Assessment & workload

1 unit, S/NC, attendance only; no assignments.

## Who it's for

Anyone — students, engineers, and researchers who want a low-cost way to track the frontier; also useful as group-meeting or course supplementary material.

## Study advice

You can watch the YouTube recordings without enrolling. Write down three takeaways per talk, or you will forget them.

> Since their introduction in 2017, Transformers have revolutionized Deep Learning, powering large language models (LLM) like ChatGPT and DeepSeek, image and video generation (e.g. Dall-E and Sora), and much more. In CS25, one of Stanford's hottest seminar courses, we delve into Transformer architectures and their diverse applications through classroom discussions and instructor and guest lectures. We host leading researchers, with past speakers like Andrej Karpathy, Geoffrey Hinton, Jim Fan, Ashish Vaswani, and folks from OpenAI, Anthropic, Google DeepMind, NVIDIA, etc. Lectures are livestreamed and recorded, attracting a global audience with over a million total YouTube views. This is a 1-unit S/NC course, where attendance is the only homework! Enroll via Axess or audit through livestreams or in-person, space permitting.
