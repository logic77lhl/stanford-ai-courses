## Course positioning

CS 229 is one of Stanford's most classic and influential machine-learning courses, and it goes noticeably deeper than CS 230. It is less about how to call a model and more about the assumptions, losses, optimization, and generalization behind it — the foundation for research or a PhD.

## What it covers

- **Supervised learning**: linear/logistic regression, GLMs, SVMs, kernels, trees and ensembles
- **Learning theory**: bias-variance, regularization, feature selection, generalization and model selection
- **Unsupervised learning**: k-means, GMMs, EM, PCA, ICA, density estimation
- **Deep learning basics**: neural networks, backpropagation, training techniques
- **Reinforcement learning**: MDPs, value iteration, policy search, adaptive control
- **Course project**: usually an open-ended ML research or application project

## Assessment & workload

Typically 4–5 problem sets plus a midterm and a final project; about 10–15 hours per week. Check the current syllabus for exact weights.

## Who it's for

People who want to understand the math behind ML and later do research or a PhD; engineers who already use scikit-learn but want the derivations and theory; interview candidates who want more than memorized answers.

## Study advice

Write the problem-set derivations yourself, especially GLMs, the SVM dual, and EM. Pair it with CS 229M or STATS 315A to fill in the theory side.
